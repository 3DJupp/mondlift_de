A wheel that stands up

The Mondlift is a travelling fairground ride of the Enterprise type, made by Huss of Bremen: twenty freely swinging gondolas on a wheel of about twenty-two metres that rises during the ride from horizontal to vertical and carries its riders upside down, with no lap bar and no belt. As far as is publicly known, it is the last ride of this design still moving from funfair to funfair in Germany.

From 1981 the ride was owned by the Zehle family of showmen from Munich; before that it ran in the Netherlands under the name Moonlift. Since the end of the 2023 season it has belonged to Zettl Enterprises KG. The year it was built is not clearly documented: the sources say 1976 or 1978, and that is how it stays here.

This site is a private reference work: a fact sheet of the ride with its history, the Enterprise design from Anton Schwarzkopf in 1972 to today's versions, a glossary of the terms and a page of questions and sources. Every figure says where it comes from; where the sources contradict each other, both values are given.

The English pages are translations of the German original. Names of places, companies and funfairs stay in German.

About the animation above

The picture in the background is computed, not filmed. The ride runs the way it does on site: the wheel spins up flat above the platform until the gondolas swing out, then the boom lifts it about its joint behind the ticket booth to 87 degrees, holds it at the top and lowers it again. Every gondola points in the direction of the resultant force of centrifugal force and gravity. Flat and slow, the gondolas hang down; steep and fast, they stand out radially, without a single special case in the code.

The mural on the back wall is the only image in the scene that is not computed. It is based on the Planet Coaster model of the Mondlift by Chaos and shows the paintwork of the real ride.
