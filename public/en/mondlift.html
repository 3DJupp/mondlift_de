The Mondlift

A wheel of twenty-two metres, twenty gondolas on it, and a movement that looks flat and harmless at first: the Mondlift is a travelling fairground ride of the Enterprise type, made by Huss of Bremen, and as far as is publicly known the only transportable ride of this design still on the road in Germany.

The space required is given as 22 by 22 metres, so the ride needs a square of almost half a football pitch and a power supply that would serve a row of terraced houses.

How the ride runs

The order is engineering, not showmanship. When riders board, the wheel lies almost flat and the gondolas hang down as on a roundabout. Then it starts and gets up to speed; for the Mondlift up to fourteen revolutions per minute are quoted. With a diameter of twenty-two metres, a gondola covers a good sixteen metres per second, just under sixty kilometres per hour.

Only then does the boom rise and tilt the wheel close to vertical. The hub climbs from a good two and a half metres to about ten, and the gondolas are carried upside down. Nobody falls out, and that is not down to a lap bar (there is none) but to the centrifugal force at the top being greater than gravity. That is why the wheel has to spin first and may only tilt afterwards. Stopping happens in reverse order.

Nobody has to steer what the gondolas do: each one lines up with the resultant force of centrifugal force and gravity. Flat and slow, they hang down; steep and fast, they stand out radially. The animation on the home page computes exactly that and needs no special case for it.

From Moonlift to Mondlift

In almost fifty years the ride has seen three circles of owners, and a change of name right at the start.

from 1976 or 1978
The first owner is the Dutch showman Michel Ropers. The ride runs under the English name Moonlift.
from 1981
The ride passes to the Zehle family of showmen from Munich and becomes known as the Mondlift (Mond is German for moon): first with Manfred Zehle, from 2014 with Petra Zehle and the Mondlift GmbH.
since October 2023
The owner is Zettl Enterprises KG from Munich. The change came at the end of the 2023 season.

The year it was built is unclear

Here the sources disagree, and this reference work leaves it that way instead of settling on one figure: the ride-index database says 1976, other directories and reports say 1978. The same uncertainty runs through the first ownership, given either as 1976 to 1981 or as 1978 to 1981. Collectors quote the serial number 31 053; the question could only really be settled through the manufacturer's records.

Two particulars

The last travelling Enterprise in Germany

The design can still be found at several amusement parks, but there it is fixed in place. As a transportable ride moving from funfair to funfair, the Mondlift is, according to the German Wikipedia, the only Huss Enterprise still operating in Germany. That makes it one of the most photographed rides of all among funfair enthusiasts.

It can run backwards

Unlike most rides of this design, the Mondlift can reverse its direction of rotation. For the riders that changes the order of the forces and with it the whole feel of the ride.

Where it has been

This site deliberately keeps no calendar. Where a travelling ride stands next season is decided through pitch allocation, that is the application to the organiser and the organiser's decision, and that is only reliable shortly before. What is documented is the past:

  • Munich Oktoberfest: regularly from 1990, then a break of about ten years without an Enterprise at the Wiesn, and back again from 2005. Whether the ride returns after the change of ownership is reported inconsistently.
  • Hamburger Dom: several times, including the spring Dom.

For the current location, ask the organiser of the funfair in question, not this site.
